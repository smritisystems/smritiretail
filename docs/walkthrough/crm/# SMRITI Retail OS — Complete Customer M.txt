# SMRITI Retail OS — Complete Customer Management Audit, Architecture Alignment & Refactoring

**Objective:** Conduct a comprehensive end-to-end audit of the existing Customer Management module and align it with enterprise-grade retail ERP standards and the agreed SMRITI architecture.

Do not blindly create new modules, tables, APIs, screens, services or business rules. First understand what already exists. Reuse, consolidate, correct and refactor wherever necessary.

## 1. Establish Existing State — Audit Before Changes

Start by examining the entire existing implementation.

Inspect:

- Customer Master and registration workflows.
- Customer profile, contact details and addresses.
- Customer categories, groups and classifications.
- Individual, business, wholesale, distributor and corporate customers.
- Customer hierarchy, parent-child relationships and multiple contacts.
- Customer-specific pricing and price lists.
- Discount rules and customer-level overrides.
- Credit limits, payment terms and credit control.
- Customer ledger and outstanding balances.
- Sales invoices, orders, quotations and returns.
- Payment receipts, adjustments and refunds.
- Customer loyalty, rewards, wallet and CGE integration.
- Customer communication and notification history.
- Customer complaints, service requests and follow-ups.
- Customer dashboard, reports and analytics.
- Customer portal and self-service capabilities.
- Customer permissions, audit logs and tenant isolation.

Trace each feature from UI → API → Service → Database → Business Rules → Reports.

Identify:
1. Existing implementation.
2. Actual database tables and relationships.
3. Hardcoded versus database-driven functionality.
4. Duplicate or conflicting implementations.
5. Missing functionality.
6. Incomplete or disconnected integrations.
7. Business logic duplicated across frontend and backend.
8. Unused legacy code and obsolete components.

## 2. Customer Identity & Master Data Governance

Establish one authoritative Customer Identity and Master Data model.

Verify:

- Stable customer ID and tenant ownership.
- Customer code generation and uniqueness.
- GSTIN validation and duplicate detection.
- Mobile number and email validation.
- Multiple billing and shipping addresses.
- Multiple contacts where applicable.
- Customer classification and lifecycle status.
- Parent-child customer relationships.
- Customer merging and duplicate resolution.
- Active, inactive, blocked and archived states.

Never create competing customer masters.

Customer identity must remain separate from customer transactions, financial balances and derived analytics.

## 3. Database & Data Integrity Audit

Inspect the actual PostgreSQL schema, ORM models, migrations, foreign keys, indexes and constraints.

Verify:

- Tenant isolation across all customer-related tables.
- Referential integrity.
- Unique constraints and appropriate indexes.
- Correct handling of soft deletion and archival.
- Customer-to-invoice and customer-to-payment relationships.
- Historical transaction integrity.
- Customer ledger consistency.
- Data migration safety.
- Schema drift between models and database.

**Critical principle:**

Customer Master must never become the authoritative source of transactional financial state.

Financial balances must be derived from the appropriate accounting and payment ledgers. Stock and sales history must be derived from their respective transaction records.

Do not overwrite historical transactions when customer details change.

## 4. Business Rules & Integration Audit

Verify the complete customer lifecycle and its interaction with:

- Billing Engine.
- Sales and POS.
- Pricing Engine.
- Discount Engine.
- GST Engine.
- Credit Management.
- Accounting and Customer Ledger.
- Payment and Refund Engine.
- Returns and Exchanges.
- SMRITI CGE — Customer Growth Engine.
- Notification and Communication Services.
- Reporting and Analytics.

Check for conflicting rules, especially:

- Customer-profile discount versus billing discount.
- Customer-specific price versus standard price list.
- Credit limit versus outstanding balance.
- Tax-inclusive versus tax-exclusive pricing.
- Returns, refunds and loyalty-point reversals.
- Customer blocking and transaction restrictions.

Existing configuration and business rules must have clear precedence and explainable outcomes.

## 5. Customer UX & Dashboard Audit

Audit every customer-related screen before changing its design.

Focus on:

- Customer registration simplicity.
- Clear field grouping and logical field dependencies.
- Avoiding unnecessary mandatory fields.
- Duplicate customer warnings.
- Clear validation messages.
- Customer profile information hierarchy.
- Transaction history and outstanding visibility.
- Useful actions and next steps.
- Search, filtering, sorting and pagination.
- Responsive layouts.
- Accessibility and consistent design tokens.

Follow SMRITI UX governance:

- SIMPLE / HYBRID / ADVANCED modes.
- Maximum 7 Action Buttons Rule.
- Minimize unnecessary clicks and fields.
- Progressive disclosure.
- Database-driven configurations.
- Consistent SMRITI UI Configuration Engine.

**Customer Dashboard must be action-oriented.**

Users should immediately understand:
- Who is this customer?
- What is the current business relationship?
- What is pending?
- What needs attention?
- What can I do next?

Do not display unnecessary metrics or actions merely to fill dashboard space.

## 6. Customer Lifecycle & Workflow Validation

Validate the following end-to-end workflows:

1. New customer registration.
2. Existing customer identification.
3. Duplicate customer detection.
4. Customer profile updates.
5. Customer classification changes.
6. Customer-specific pricing and discounts.
7. Customer credit approval and restrictions.
8. Sales and billing.
9. Payment collection.
10. Returns and refunds.
11. Loyalty accrual and redemption.
12. Customer blocking and reactivation.
13. Customer archival.
14. Customer statement and ledger reconciliation.

Verify permissions and business rules at both frontend and backend levels.

## 7. Architecture & Engineering Standards

Align implementation with the existing SMRITI architecture and frozen governance decisions.

Enforce:

- Contract-first API design.
- Separation of UI, application services and domain logic.
- Adapter isolation.
- Single source of truth.
- Multi-tenant data isolation.
- Reusable shared services.
- No duplicate business logic.
- No hardcoded configuration where configuration is required.
- Proper API validation and error handling.
- Structured logging and auditability.
- Offline and synchronization compatibility wherever supported.
- Backward compatibility for existing integrations.

Reuse the existing platform services and event architecture rather than introducing competing infrastructure.

## 8. Refactoring Rules

After completing the audit, classify every finding:

- P0 — Critical data integrity, security or financial risk.
- P1 — Major business logic or workflow defect.
- P2 — UX, performance or maintainability improvement.
- P3 — Optional enhancement.

For each finding, document:

- Existing implementation.
- Problem identified.
- Evidence and affected files.
- Recommended correction.
- Database impact.
- API impact.
- UX impact.
- Regression risk.

Then prepare a dependency-aware implementation plan.

**Implement the required corrections.**

Do not:
- Rebuild functioning modules unnecessarily.
- Introduce duplicate customer tables.
- Create random fields or screens.
- Break existing APIs without migration.
- Change historical financial records.
- Remove existing functionality without impact analysis.
- Introduce new dependencies without justification.
- Mark incomplete functionality as implemented.

Verify database migrations against a safe test database before applying them to existing data.

## 11. Definition of Completion

Customer Management can only be marked complete when:

- One authoritative customer identity exists.
- Customer-related data is properly tenant-isolated.
- Database integrity is verified.
- Business rules are consistent across modules.
- Financial and transactional history remains accurate.
- UX is clear and operationally useful.
- All relevant integrations work.
- Regression tests pass.
- Documentation reflects the actual implementation.

Work in the existing development branch.

First perform a read-only audit and produce the findings and implementation plan. Then proceed with approved, evidence-backed refactoring in dependency order without waiting for another prompt for each individual correction.

Preserve working functionality, existing data and frozen architecture decisions.

Do not stop after writing recommendations. Implement, test, verify and document the actual changes.

At completion, provide a concise engineering summary distinguishing:

1. Already compliant.
2. Refactored.
3. Newly implemented.
4. Not implemented and why.
5. Tests passed and failed.

**The objective is not to build more Customer features. The objective is to make the existing Customer Management system correct, consistent, maintainable and enterprise-ready.**
